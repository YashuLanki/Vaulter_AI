Pinal’s water constraint broke four months ago. The area’s water utility received a 100-year Assured Water Supply designation covering Coolidge and Casa Grande — the first in the Pinal Active Management Area in over two decades, enough to support an estimated 80,000 homes. Residential platting there had been stalled on groundwater limits. Every Coolidge-area listing above was put on the market before that decision, and has been sitting 430–1,700 days. That gap is the opportunity.

Sewer did not break with it. Coolidge treats 2 million gallons a day — roughly 8–10,000 homes, not 80,000. Access to the trunk line, not water, is now the constraint that decides these deals.

What each factor measures

Proximity
Distance to the nearest Vaulter holding. Clustering is the firm’s most consistent behaviour — roughly 34 of 57 holdings sit in one of 15 clusters. Where the firm owns nothing nearby this factor goes neutral rather than counting against a listing, so an unfamiliar market isn’t penalised for being unfamiliar.
Pricing
Not whether the asking price is fair against comparable sales — Vaulter is not a builder. It asks what the entitled position must sell for to return 3×, and compares that to what the market actually pays for parcels of the size this would be subdivided into.
Distress
Long time on market, a lender or REO owner, an asking price at or below what the seller paid. Counted as favourable, not risky: a distressed basis was the stated reason behind one of the firm’s best-returning acquisitions.
Size
Judged in context. Small is normal inside a cluster; large is normal as an assemblage. Only one combination scores badly — large and isolated in a market with no presence, the pattern behind 41 California foreclosures.
Inputs and their status

The weakest inputs are the four weights above. Every cost figure here is measured from the firm’s own budgets and settlement statements, and anything with no record — entitlement cost for non-residential land, most obviously — is left out and declared on the row rather than estimated. The weights are the exception: two document searches found nothing in the corpus that ranks or weights selection factors, so they are a judgement nobody has signed off.

No horizontal development cost is carried at all — streets, utilities, grading. That is deliberate: the firm sells entitled rather than improved land, and the only measured figures are Pinal County. Where a listing shows unusually generous headroom, the comparison is probably against improved land while the subject is raw, and the row says so.

What the portfolio can say about these markets

The ranking is computed from this file and travels anywhere. Whether there is any of the firm’s own history to read it against is a separate question, and this is the answer to it. An unevidenced market still ranks normally — marking it down would rank the firm’s data coverage rather than the deals.

What this deliberately does not do
Eliminate
Nothing is filtered out. Measured on a 216-row Arizona export, the earlier hard-filtering version discarded 69 listings on rules the firm’s own deal history contradicts — 46 for flood risk and 14 for having a structure on site. Eleven of those sat within three miles of land the firm already owns.
Rank on type
Residential, commercial, industrial and agricultural all compete. Single deals have spanned several.
Judge the market
Whether a jurisdiction can actually deliver sewer, annexation or a plat is researched separately, per jurisdiction. See the Coolidge file.
